Vignettes and tolls in Europe: how not to overpay or get fined

Checked on 7 November 2026

Europe uses three main systems. Some countries sell a vignette for a period of time, others charge per kilometre or per section, and the rest are free for cars or charge only for a bridge, a tunnel or a short section. Prices for every country are in the tolls and vignettes tab on the TankSmart homepage. This page covers the rules that catch drivers out.

Which system where

SystemCountries

In Moldova the vignette, and in Belarus the BelToll electronic toll, apply to cars registered abroad (in Belarus, outside the Eurasian Economic Union). Since 1 January 2026 Iceland charges a kilometre fee for every vehicle. Rental companies pass it on to the renter, and if you bring your own car, customs charges a fixed fee on arrival, ISK 13,900 for a stay of up to 10 days.

Most vignettes are digital and tied to the number plate

In most vignette countries there is no sticker any more: cameras read the number plate and check it against the database. Enter the plate exactly as it is on the car and pick the right country. In Hungary a plate with up to three wrong characters can be corrected for HUF 1,470, and mixing up 0 and O or 1 and I is corrected free. If a surcharge notice has already arrived, apply within 60 days. In Moldova a wrong plate or VIN counts as driving without a vignette. In Austria the 2026 sticker stays valid until 31 January 2027, but the 2027 vignette, valid from 1 December 2026, is digital only.

Buy only on the official website

Unofficial websites sell the same vignettes with a markup, and some never deliver a valid vignette at all. Austria's ASFINAG, Slovenia's DARS and the Swiss National Cyber Security Centre all warn about them, and Czechia moved its official shop to a gov.cz address to protect drivers from look-alike sites. In 2022 the European Consumer Centre in Czechia described a case where a 10-day vignette cost €19.80 instead of €9.80. Such sites often appear first in search results as paid ads. The official shops are:

  • Austria: shop.asfinag.at
  • Bulgaria: bgtoll.bg
  • Czechia: edalnice.gov.cz
  • Hungary: ematrica.nemzetiutdij.hu
  • Moldova: vinieta.gov.md
  • Romania: etoll.ro
  • Slovakia: eznamka.sk
  • Slovenia: evinjeta.dars.si
  • Switzerland: via.admin.ch

Austria: the 18-day rule

An annual or 2-month digital vignette that you buy online from ASFINAG is only valid from the 18th day after purchase, because consumers have a right to withdraw from online purchases. The 1-day and 10-day vignettes bought online are valid right away. A vignette bought at a sales point, for example at a petrol station or an ÖAMTC office, can be valid immediately. If you are leaving soon and want a longer vignette, buy it at a sales point.

Tolls on top of the vignette

In Austria some routes cost extra even with a vignette: the Brenner motorway (A13) €12.50, the Tauern motorway (A10) €15, the Arlberg tunnel (S16) €13, and the Bosruck and Gleinalm tunnels on the Pyhrn route (A9) €7 and €12. The Karawanken tunnel between Austria and Slovenia (A11) costs €9. Near some Austrian borders there are short vignette-free stretches: the A1 from Walserberg to Salzburg Nord, the A12 from the German border at Kufstein to Kufstein Süd and the A14 from Hörbranz to Hohenems.

No booths: paying afterwards

  • France: on the A13, A14, A69 and A79 and at the A4 Boulay exit there are no barriers. Unless you have a toll badge, you have 72 hours to pay on the operator's website or at an approved payment point, such as a Nirio tobacconist. After a notice, you pay a €10 fee within 15 days, €90 after that, and after two months a €375 fine.
  • Italy: the yellow Telepass lanes are only for cars with an on-board unit. If you drive into one by mistake, don't reverse: press the help button. If you can't pay at the booth or your card fails, you get a "Mancato pagamento" receipt. Pay it within 15 days, online at Autostrade per l'Italia or on the website printed on the receipt.
  • Croatia: you still take a ticket and pay at the exit. From 1 March 2027 tolls will be collected without barriers, with an ENC device or a registered number plate.
  • Portugal: some motorways only have electronic tolls. With a foreign plate, link a bank card to the plate or buy a TollCard at a CTT post office. The A22 in the Algarve and several other former SCUT motorways have been free since 1 January 2025.
  • Norway: there are no booths. Register the car with Epass24 before the trip, otherwise you get an invoice later with an extra fee for looking up the owner.
  • Ireland and the UK: the M50 around Dublin is barrier-free, pay at eflow.ie. The Dartford Crossing near London has no booths either. Pay at gov.uk/dart-charge by midnight the next day, or you get a £70 penalty, halved to £35 if you pay it within 14 days.

Fines for driving without a vignette

  • Austria: a €200 replacement toll since 2026. If you don't pay it, €300 to €3,000.
  • Switzerland: CHF 200, plus the CHF 40 vignette.
  • Slovenia: €300. If you don't contest the penalty notice, you pay half, as long as you pay within 8 days after it becomes final. Drivers who live abroad can be told to pay on the spot, and then there is no discount.
  • Czechia: up to CZK 5,000 on the spot and up to CZK 20,000 in administrative proceedings.

Not a toll, but you may need it: environmental stickers

Germany has about 35 environmental zones, and all of them require the green sticker (Umweltplakette), foreign cars included. Without it the fine is €100. You can get it from registration offices and inspection bodies such as TÜV or Dekra, or online from ADAC, for about €5 to €20. In France, Paris, Lyon, Grenoble, Marseille, Toulouse, Strasbourg, Montpellier and other cities have low-emission zones that require the Crit'Air sticker. Order it only at certificat-air.gouv.fr, where no extra fees are charged. Without it you can be fined €68 or €135, depending on the vehicle. Some zones, including Greater Paris, are not issuing fines yet, but the sticker is still required.

Before you go

Check the tolls and vignettes tab on TankSmart for prices and where to buy in each country. If your route crosses a country with a vignette, buy it before you reach the border, on the official site or at a sales point, and keep the receipt until the trip is over.
